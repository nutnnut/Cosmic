Bot Self-Scrolling

How a bot decides what gear to scroll and with which scroll — owner-confirmed, one item at a time.

Companion-scope feature: a bot improves its own equipment using scrolls it already owns, only through legal means a player could also use. This page describes the shipped behavior (branch experimental).

1. Talking to the bot

Everything starts from owner chat. Three commands, all plain ASCII:

You sayWhat happens
scroll nowOne-shot: scan gear + owned scrolls, propose the single best play (or explain why there isn't one).
scroll on / scroll my gearArm self-scrolling (selfScrollEnabled) and propose the first play. After each confirmed scroll it automatically looks for the next one.
scroll offDisarm and cancel any pending proposal.
scroll debugDump the full decision to logs/bot-scroll/scroll-debug-<name>.txt: every scroll in the bot's bag, every candidate equip with its meso value, the chosen play, and the reproduction-cost table for that piece (see §6).
yes / noAnswer a pending proposal. Every scroll needs a yes before it's applied; anything that isn't a clear yes cancels it.
you: scroll now
bot: want me to use 60% Scroll for Gloves (ATT) on my Work Glove? ~60% success (yes/no)
you: yes
bot: yes! that hit, my Work Glove is better now

2. The decision pipeline

When asked for a play, the bot runs this once and proposes at most one scroll:

1
Collect candidate gear. Every equip the bot is wearing plus every wearable equip in its bag (non-cash). Bag items are kept only if the bot can actually equip them — right job/level/stat requirements, and for weapons a compatible weapon type. (So it never burns scrolls on gear it can't use.)
2
Keep only scrollable gear. Each candidate must have at least one free upgrade slot.
3
Match owned scrolls to each item. For every equip, find the scrolls in the bot's USE inventory that legally apply to it (see §4).
4
Drop out-classed gear (dominance). If the bot already owns enough strictly-better items for that slot to fill it, scrolling this one is pointless — skip it (see §5).
5
Score every (item, scroll) pair by its expected value and pick the single best one (see §6).
6
Propose & confirm. The winning pair becomes a yes/no chat prompt. On yes the scroll is applied through the exact same path a player uses; the result (success / fail / boom) is announced and nearby bots react.

3. Which gear is even considered

Worn gear

Always eligible — it's already equipped, so it's obviously usable. Must have a free upgrade slot.

Bag gear

Eligible only if the bot could wear it (job/level/stat reqs + weapon type) — checked without side effects. This is what lets a bot scroll a fresh spare up toward something it'll equip later.

Each item is bucketed by its equipment slot (hat, weapon, ring, …) so it can be compared against its real alternatives. Item IDs are not unique — a bot can own two copies of the same item with different stats/slots — so the system tracks the exact item instance, never re-looks-up by ID.

4. Which scroll applies to an item

A scroll is offered for an equip only if all of these hold:

CheckRule
Is a stat scrollItem is in the scroll range and is not a Clean Slate, stat-modifier, or White Scroll.
Applies to this itemThe scroll's explicit item list contains this equip, or (no list) the scroll category matches the equip category.
Has upsideListed success chance > 0 and it adds stats the bot's class actually values.
Won't destroy gear v1Any scroll with a boom (cursed) chance is currently skipped by the scanner — see §8.

5. Dominance — "do I already have something better?"

Older logic blocked any gear the bot had out-leveled. That wrongly benched a low-level item that's still your best. The replacement compares against real alternatives:

An item is out-classed when ≥ capacity other same-slot items are each
  • strictly better as-is  AND  • have ≥ as many upgrade slots.
capacity = how many of that slot you wear at once → 4 for rings, 1 for most.
Key nuance. A weaker base with more slots is not out-classed — its scroll potential can still overtake a maxed-out rival. That's exactly the upgrade we want to chase.

Example

Worn glove: 20 STR, 0 slots (maxed). Spare in bag: 15 STR, 5 slots.

The spare is worse right now but has slots, so it is not dominated → it becomes a scroll target and can be built past the worn one. The worn glove has 0 slots, so it's simply not scrollable.

Rings: because you wear four at once, an item is only benched when four better rings exist — your 2nd/3rd-best ring is still worth improving.

6. Scoring: what to scroll, with what

The bot picks the (item, scroll) play that gains the most meso-value. Everything is denominated in meso, so "is this scroll worth it?" is a like-for-like comparison.

6.1  Value of an item = its reproduction cost

An item is worth the cheapest expected meso to reproduce one this good from scratch. To make a base reach a target stat-score you buy a clean base and scroll it; a regular scroll burns a slot win-or-lose, so the only "retry" is at the item level — a base that runs out of slots below target (or booms) is discarded and a fresh one bought. The cheapest-production policy is a small DP over (slots left, score) with an explicit abandon-and-rebuy option:

value(s, a) = min(
   rebuy:   B + D   (abandon this base; D = expected cost of a fresh one)
   each scroll i:   costi + pi·value(s−1, a+gaini) + (1−pi)·value(s−1, a) )

The resulting value-vs-score curve is flat at the clean-base cost and convex above it (the binomial upper tail under a fixed slot budget makes the last points exponentially dearer).

Why convex matters. Convexity is exactly what makes the bot snowball a strong base and abandon a crippled one — and it makes the rare/big gamble go on the first slot with an immediate cut-loss if it misses ("gamble early, cut losses early"). These behaviors emerge from the math; none are hand-coded thresholds.

The clean-base offense score is class-weighted: 5 × ATT + 1 × main-stat + 0.3 × secondary (mage uses magic ATT, INT/LUK). This is a transparent v1 stand-in for the full equip optimizer.

6.2  The online decision

For each scrollable, non-dominated equip the bot compares value if it starts scrolling now (running the same DP over the item's free slots) against value as-is. The difference is the expected meso it would gain. It proposes the single (item, scroll) pair with the largest gain.

improvement = bestAchievableValue(free slots) − valueNow  ·  propose argmax over all candidates

Effective odds, not catalog odds. The success chance fed to the DP is the rate the server will actually roll — the WZ success plus the server's SCROLL_SUCCESS_BONUS (capped at 100), mirroring the real scroll engine. A 10% scroll on a +10-bonus server is planned as 20%.

6.3  Cost & rarity → meso how prices are derived

Both the value curve and the scroll cost need a meso figure for every scroll and base. The intended model is rarity → meso: how hard a thing is to pull out of the world (drop-rate × farming effort). Here is what is actually computed today vs. what is still designed:

ThingCost todayNotes
ScrollCheapest legitimate NPC-shop buy price (shopitems), else a flat 1,000,000 stub if drop-only.The full price is used for the value curve; the per-apply cost is 0.9 × price (scrolls are liquid, so using one forgoes nearly its full resale).
Base itemmin(NPC-shop price if sold,  max(100k, reqLevel × 10k) drop-farm stub).Cheapest source wins. A rare drop an NPC sells cheaply is correctly priced at the NPC price.
GM / junk shops are filtered out. Some shops list valuables for 1 meso. A legitimate shop never sells an item below its NPC sell-back value (that would be free arbitrage), so any listing with buyPrice ≤ sellBack is dropped before taking the cheapest price.
What "rarity" means today — be honest. There is no drop-rate-based rarity math yet. "Rarity" currently reduces to market price when known (the NPC shop, GM-filtered) and a crude level-scaled placeholder otherwise. The real model — cost = (1 / dropRate) × effortPerKill, returning ∞ when content is unreachable — is designed in economy-design.md §4 (FarmingCostModel) but not built. When it lands it simply replaces the two stubs above; nothing else changes.
Godly (better-than-clean) bases. A dropped base has a GODLY_STATS_DROP_CHANCE to roll bonus stats, making it better than catalog. These are not averaged into the clean floor (the cheapest source — often an NPC — gives clean stats). They are higher-score products valued by their own drop source — a separate acquisition path up the curve, deferred until the rarity→meso model and the roll distribution exist.

6.4  Extra gate for boom-capable scrolls

A destroy-capable scroll is allowed only when both: a usable fallback item exists for the slot (so a boom is recoverable), and the expected gain stays positive after the boom-loss term. (Logic is built and unit-tested; the scanner just doesn't feed boom scrolls yet — §8.)

7. Applying the scroll

On yes, after a short delay, the bot re-validates (item & scroll still present, slot still free) and then applies the scroll through the same engine a player's scroll uses. Outcomes:

ResultMeaningBot reaction
SUCCESSStat applied / level up"yes! that hit, my <item> is better now"
FAILSlot consumed, no gain"aw, failed - lost a slot"
BOOMItem destroyed (boom scrolls only)"noo it boomed..." — worn item is removed

The scroll is consumed, inventory/visuals update, the scroll effect is broadcast on the map, and nearby bots react. If self-scrolling is armed and the item survived, the bot looks for its next play after a beat.

8. When it proposes nothing

Instead of a flat "no", the bot gives the most specific reason it can:

SituationBot says
No worn/bag item has a free slot"nothing to scroll - my gear's out of upgrade slots"
No owned scroll fits any slotted item"i dont have any scrolls that fit my gear"
Only boom-risk scrolls fit"i only have boom-risk scrolls, ill skip those for now"
Scrolls fit but add nothing useful"those scrolls wouldnt add anything useful for me"
Usable scrolls, but only on out-classed gear"i already have better gear for those slots, saving the scrolls"
Everything fits but the odds don't pay"not worth it - the odds dont pay off, ill save the scrolls"

9. Current limits & what's next

v1 safety cut. The scanner only proposes non-destroying scrolls, so this build cannot destroy the bot's gear. The boom-scroll decision logic exists and is tested; wiring it in (with fallback-slot detection) is a later increment.

Known gaps

  • Rarity is price + stub, not drop-rate — cost = GM-filtered NPC price when known, else a level-scaled placeholder. The real (1/dropRate) × effort model (§6.3) isn't built.
  • Offense-only value — HP/DEF/avoid/accuracy/utility score 0, so defensive scrolls are never used.
  • No live market — the value curve uses owned scrolls + NPC prices, not bot-driven supply/demand prices (the economy capstone).
  • No periodic re-scan — when armed it only chains after a confirmed scroll, not on a timer as it farms new scrolls.
  • Toggle not persisted across relog.

Where this lives in code

server.bots.BotScrollPlanner — pure decision core: per-candidate improvement over the value curve, dominance gate, boom gate. Unit-tested in isolation.

server.bots.BotScrollManager — wiring: gathers gear & scrolls, slot/wearability/dominance, builds the value curve (NPC prices, GM filter, effective odds), applies the scroll, "nothing to scroll" reasons, the debug export.
