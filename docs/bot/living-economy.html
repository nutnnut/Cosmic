Living Bot Economy

How companion bots discover prices by trading — no central price-setter, no authored price tables, no rate caps. Just agents that farm, scroll, list, buy, and re-price.

This is the shipped behavior as of 2026-07-11 (slices S0–S4, the 2026-07-04 scroll/market audit fixes, and the supply/demand price-discovery pass). The as-built map and invariants live in docs/bot/economy.md; the price-discovery mechanism notes are in docs/bot/kb/kb_bot_market_price_discovery.md (the original design docs were consolidated; the rev-2 design of record survives in git history at fa5f59405). This page explains what actually runs and why, every claim tied to a real class/method.

1. Big picture — a decentralized, belief-based market

There is no server that decides prices. Every bot privately values goods, privately believes what the market pays, and acts on those beliefs. The clearing price is whatever their trades settle at. The only shared piece of machinery is a damped statistic — a per-item consensus computed from the trade tape — and even that never places an order or clamps anyone. It just gives everyone a rough shared idea that individual perception then distorts and personal experience then corrects.

SHARED — MySQL tape
BotMarketLedger → bot_market_event. Append-only record of every arms-length event: TRADE, STALL_SALE, LIST, SHOUT, NPC_SELL/BUY, faucet/sink counters, plus the censored exposure outcomes UNSOLD and SOLD_FAST. It records; it never creates meso or items.
▼  3-minute off-tick sweep
SHARED — the stabilizer
BotMarketConsensus. Per priceKey, a recency-weighted median of realized clearing prices (TRADE / STALL_SALE only — LIST and SHOUT advertisements are audit data and can never seed or move it), damped by decayed event volume. Exposed-stock outcomes (UNSOLD / SOLD_FAST) then apply bounded directional pressure on top (§4). One odd trade barely moves a liquid item but meaningfully informs an illiquid one; the median shrugs off gift-price outliers. A statistic, not a setter.
▼  noisy, lagged sample (only at market touchpoints)
PER BOT — the private book (tick-thread-owned)
BotMarketBook. Blends the bot's own observations (its completed trades weighted 1.0, asks it browsed 0.4, shouts 0.15…) with a noisy, lagged sample of consensus. The noise is seeded stable per (bot, key, day) — a bot holds a consistently slightly-wrong idea for a session rather than jittering. Social bots are better informed; loners are wronger. This is the only price source any decision may read.
▼
PER BOT — decisions
List / buy / use / scroll. WTP, reservation, the upgrade menu, the scroll DP — all run off the private book, never the raw ledger or raw consensus.
Two hard rules that keep it honest. (1) Player↔player trades never feed pricing — only bots' own books observe, and they observe only events they were party to or physically browsed. (2) A stall sale fires the tape and belief update even when the seller is a player (HiredMerchant.buy → BotManager.notifyStallSale), because a bot browsing a map can't tell a player stall from a bot stall — it just reads getItems() of whatever merchants stand there.

No decision code may read channel-wide merchant registries, the raw ledger, the raw consensus, or another bot's book. The shared consensus reaches behavior only through the noisy, touchpoint-sampled perception in BotMarketBook. Enforced by convention: the book is the single price source for behavior.

2. How a bot values a scroll

A scroll's meso worth is a stack of three independent numbers — a supply anchor, a demand ceiling, and (only when using one) an opportunity cost. Each answers a different question.

LayerQuestionHow it's computed
Supply anchor
scrollPriceMeso		 What would it cost to get another one? The cheapest legit source, now blended with the live tape:
  • Shop-sold → min(NPC shop price, consensus) when the tape has evidence, else the shop price. The NPC's infinite supply is a hard cap, but a glut trading below shop passes straight through.
  • Drop-only → the consensus when the tape has clearings, else the farm cost (farmingCostMeso: best-dropper drop-rate × realistic time-to-kill for this bot × FARM_MESO_PER_SECOND, plus spawn-density seek overhead).
  • Chaos / White → max(10M cold-market floor, consensus) — now that chaos gambles and whites protect, the market prices them; the 10M is only a floor until the tape has clearings.
Demand ceiling
scrollCombatCeilingMeso		 What's the most a best-buyer pays for the combat value it injects? SCROLL_CEILING_PER_EV × success × marketStatValue × slotDurabilityFactor. Crucially marketStatValue now scores a multi-stat scroll by its best-use worth across class camps (bestRoleWorth), not the sum of all four mains — so a +15-all-stat scroll is valued at what its best buyer gets (~19.5), not 60.
Opportunity cost of USING
applyCostMeso		 What do I forgo by burning this one instead of selling it? 0.9 × scrollPriceMeso — a scroll is liquid, so consuming one forgoes nearly its resale. Zero for tradeBlocked scrolls ([4yrAnniv], Dragon Stone): they can't be sold/listed/traded, so consuming one forgoes no sale. The reproduction curve still keeps the full price — remaking one still costs effort; only this action cost is zeroed.
The slotDurabilityFactor is why glove scrolls cost more than weapon scrolls. It's WZ-derived: least-squares growth of marketStatValue per required level within a slot category, normalized so the weapon average is 1.0. Flat-progression slots (gloves ~2.6×, shoes ~2.6×, capes ~2.4×) scale up because a scrolled flat-slot piece stays best-in-slot near-forever, where a scrolled weapon is soon outgrown. The same +2 ATT is simply worth more where it lasts. Behavior follows price for free: because per-apply cost is a fraction of this ceiling, bots are proportionally more reluctant to burn a flat-slot scroll on a marginal gain.

GM/junk shops are filtered before any of this: a legitimate shop never sells an item below its NPC sell-back value (free arbitrage), so any shopitems row with buy ≤ sell-back is dropped by marketBuyPriceMeso's reverse index.

3. How a bot decides to scroll

Valuation feeds the same decision engine the self-scrolling pipeline documents in full. In one paragraph: an item is worth the cheapest expected meso to reproduce one this good from scratch — a small DP over (slots left, score) with an explicit abandon-and-rebuy option (BotScrollValuer.reproductionValue). Because a regular scroll burns a slot win-or-lose, the only retry is at the item level, which makes the value-vs-score curve convex above the clean-base cost. The planner (BotScrollPlanner.planBest) picks the single best (item, scroll) play, applies it, then rescans — so snowballing a strong base and abandoning a crippled one is emergent, not scripted. Boom scrolls are gated on a usable fallback item for the slot plus positive EV after the boom-loss term. Chaos is a Monte-Carlo-free path — an exact convolution over the piece's own convex band curve. White is protected when failRate × p × marginal-band value > white cost.

Nothing here is a threshold someone typed in. "Gamble early, cut losses early", "snowball winners", "protect only when the slot at risk is worth more than the white" — these all fall out of the convex curve and the EV comparison. See self-scrolling.html for the DP, the worked example, and the chat flow.

4. Feedback loops & self-correction

This is the centerpiece. Prices don't stay right because anyone corrects them — they stay right because every mispricing creates the pressure that erases it. The core loop, both directions:

GLUT — too many listings, price too high
many listings → stalls sit unsold through the ~hourly service visit → repriceUnsold steps the ask down (pressure grows with repeated exposure and same-key stock) and tapes an UNSOLD outcome → lower clearings and the UNSOLD pressure pull consensus down → applyCostMeso falls, so bots burn more scrolls; and maybeBargainBuy clears cheap stock → supply drains → price recovers
SCARCITY — nothing for sale, price too low
wants go unfilled → repeated failure raises the belief (scarcity evidence, but WTP stays capped by use-value) → stock that sells within 15 min of listing/repricing tapes SOLD_FAST, probing consensus one step up → consensus rises → farming that item beats the alternatives (grind steering, S4) → supply returns → price settles at ≈ frontier cost

The load-bearing piece is the unsold-pressure undercut. Without it the market froze: every ask sat a margin above every WTP (a structural no-trade zone), and worse, glut entrants who perceive consensus priced at the going rate — so gluts raised prices. Stepping the ask to just under the cheapest visible competitor (never above the bot's own perception; reservation still floors it) is what converts oversupply into falling prices.

Price healing from exposed supply and demand (2026-07-11)

Clearing-only consensus fixed the ask-echo rumor loop, but it left a hole: a persisted bad consensus at a quality band with no clearings could sit wrong forever — nothing trades at the poisoned price, so no trade ever corrects it. The fix is to let real exposure speak. A bot stall is a standing experiment: stock offered at a price that survives the ~hourly service interval (55–65 min, per-bot jittered) is evidence the price is too high; stock that clears within 15 minutes of opening or repricing is evidence it was too low. Both are censored outcomes — they bound the true price from one side without claiming to know it.

Guardrails keep outcomes from becoming advertisements. An outcome can never seed a key that has no clearing history; UNSOLD may only lower an established consensus and SOLD_FAST (probing +10% above the cleared ask) may only raise it; targets within a 5% deadband of consensus are ignored, so near-equilibrium noise does nothing; outcome weight is 0.3 vs a trade's 1.0, scales sublinearly with quantity (log₂, capped 8×), and stays volume-damped by clearing history; and a real clearing resets the seller's accumulated unsold pressure. Markdowns start small (~6% of the gap per observation, capped at 50%, never below the reservation floor of max(NPC sell-back, half the current ask) per visit) and grow only with repeated exposure or crowded same-key stock.

Design lineage: the rev-2 design allowed listing asks as weak consensus evidence when no clearings existed, and kept sold-fast/unsold outcomes private to each bot's book. Both were revised in July 2026 — ask evidence created a self-reinforcing rumor loop (reproduction-cost asks seeded the consensus that justified the next generation of high asks), and private-only outcomes could never heal a persisted bad consensus that every book keeps re-sampling. The 0.3 outcome weight the design planned is kept, applied at the shared layer with the bounds above.

Why it converges instead of oscillating. Demand is price-elastic (WTP caps), supply is price-responsive with lag, updates are confidence-damped, repricing is asynchronous across bots (each rides its own personality schedule, so no two reprice in the same tick), and the consensus layer bounds collective error — individual perceptions orbit a slow robust statistic instead of chasing each other. A sparse market can't random-walk and a single outlier can't cascade.

5. Special scrolls — a case study

Two owner-interesting scrolls exercise the "req-locked, trade-blocked" corners. Neither needed special-casing — they flow through the same planner emergently.

[4yrAnniv] scrolls

22 ids, req-locked to Maple event gear (e.g. 2043013 → Maple Glory Sword; capes → Maple Cape), mostly cursed=30 (boom), all tradeBlock=1. Obtainable only as a 20% drop from event boss Giant Cake (9400606).

The req path is fully emergent: getScrollReqs reads the WZ req id-list, applicable() honors it (req list wins; category match is only the fallback), and the planner picks it up. Boom variants need a usable fallback piece for the slot before they're allowed. Zero opportunity cost since tradeBlocked.

Dragon Stone (2041200)

100% success, +15 all stats / +140 PDD / +140 MDD / +15 EVA, only=1, tradeBlock=1, reqRUC=3, req = Horntail Necklace. Not in drop_data; its only shop rows are price-1 GM shops that shopPrices() (price > 1) already excludes.

Applies emergently on an HTP with a free slot. Its +15 mains score a gain of ~19.5 for any job so the DP fires; but its 280 defense and +15 avoid are invisible to the offense-only gain axis (open gap 8, below).

Accessory scrolls 20492xx (pendant/ring/belt) now apply via the shared ItemConstants.canScroll (extracted from the player's ScrollHandler path — bots share player code) instead of a reimplemented category fallback. But every obtainable one is cursed=50 (boom), and the market catalog index excludes boom scrolls by design, so accessory band curves stay flat until a non-boom accessory scroll exists.
